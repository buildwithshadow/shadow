# Shadow Accelerator Roadmap

Status: locked on 2026-09-02 for the Arc accelerator.

Shadow lets organizations delegate USDC authority to autonomous agents under enforceable limits. Today that means paying approved providers through Float. After Float reaches its operating and customer-evidence gate, Shadow expands the same mandate primitive to policy-controlled protocol liquidity.

The roadmap contains two sequential paths. Path B is not a fallback or a competing idea. Shadow completes Path A first, then advances to Path B one validated operation and one design partner at a time.

## Two-Month Outcome

By the end of the accelerator, Shadow must be able to show:

- a repeatable, independently operated Float lifecycle;
- a credible reason for sponsors to fund reusable agent spending capacity;
- a defined revenue, reserve, and loss-bearing model;
- one committed design partner for the next mandate-controlled capital action;
- one bounded real-protocol adapter only if that partner supplies a concrete workflow and pilot commitment;
- a security and operating path appropriate for Arc mainnet;
- an investor-ready story supported by usage, commitments, and onchain evidence rather than infrastructure claims.

## Path A — Prove Shadow Float

Float is the accelerator starting product and the first commercial proof.

### Product loop

1. A sponsor reserves USDC for an agent.
2. The sponsor defines provider, endpoint, amount, daily, expiry, and executor limits.
3. The agent signs a bounded EIP-712 purchase intent.
4. `ShadowFloat` pays the approved provider directly or refuses the request before funds move.
5. Successful payment opens agent debt onchain.
6. Repayment restores available capacity.
7. The sponsor may update policy or reclaim unused reserve after debt reaches zero.

### Target customer

An agent platform, protocol, or business that wants agents to purchase APIs, data, compute, or services without pre-funding every agent wallet or granting unrestricted control of organizational funds.

### Evidence already available

- a deployed Arc testnet Float contract and public product;
- permissionless sponsor-funded lines;
- contract-verified signed provider purchases;
- debt, repayment, restored capacity, refusal, default, and reserve-reclaim accounting;
- 14 signed provider payments and 13 repaid lifecycles in the live V2 activity set as of 2026-09-02;
- one verified externally sponsored lifecycle;
- a public API and 26-check no-secret verifier;
- 2,893 historical policy and receipt records from Shadow's earlier copy-capital system.

Historical copy trading is evidence that the mandate primitive came from a real delegation use case. It is not Shadow's company framing.

### Path A customer questions

- Why is a reusable line better than pre-funding an agent wallet?
- Which providers does the agent purchase from repeatedly?
- Who funds reserves and who absorbs defaults?
- Which policy limits are indispensable?
- What fee will a sponsor pay and what recurring value justifies it?
- Does repayment-backed restored capacity change sponsor or agent behaviour?

### Path A completion gate

Path A is complete only when all of the following are evidenced:

- one external sponsor and agent complete three unassisted spend-and-repay cycles on separate occasions;
- the same pilot produces one genuine policy refusal with no provider transfer;
- the agent independently clears its debt;
- the sponsor independently reclaims the remaining reserve;
- at least one target organization confirms a recurring purchasing workflow and continues into a pilot or integration commitment;
- Float's fee, reserve source, default owner, and loss limits are written from customer evidence;
- security, monitoring, incident response, and Arc mainnet dependencies have named owners and release gates.

Assisted transactions, founder-controlled wallets, test counts, and historical receipts do not satisfy this gate.

### Path A work order

1. Rehearse and deliver the three-minute accelerator presentation using the live product's current evidence.
2. Complete the unassisted sponsor-agent-provider pilot.
3. Interview agent platforms, service providers, protocol teams, and prospective sponsors around repeated purchasing behaviour.
4. Fix only the onboarding, reliability, and integration issues that block those users.
5. Freeze the economic model and initial customer profile from observed behaviour.
6. Prepare the sponsored-only production candidate for independent review and Arc mainnet operations.

## Considering — Two Separate Float Extensions

Recorded 2026-09-08. Status: idea parked for founder consideration, not approved for implementation or a change to the locked roadmap.

These are two distinct ideas to evaluate independently, not one bundled feature or a requirement to build both.

### Idea 1 — Delivery Evidence and Verification

Explore how Float can establish whether the purchased service was delivered and met agreed requirements. Define the evidence, acceptance criteria, and verifier for a narrow service. This can complement the existing direct-payment flow without introducing refunds or escrow.

The current contract has provider-signed delivery receipts tied to paid requests. These are evidence of the provider's claim, not independent verification of correctness, escrow, or guaranteed refunds.

### Idea 2 — Refunds and Non-Delivery Recourse

Explore how a customer can receive money back when a provider fails to deliver or an agreed refund condition applies. Evaluate provider-issued refunds as separate transfers and, separately, whether escrow or another explicitly funded mechanism is warranted. Returning funds held in escrow is distinct from recovering a payment already released to a provider. Shadow cannot unilaterally reverse a finalized provider payment; refund authority, funding, and enforcement must be defined rather than assumed.

This idea can be evaluated independently of automated delivery verification, using agreed evidence or a defined manual process. Neither idea commits Shadow to a general dispute-resolution product.

Before promoting either idea, establish the relevant requirements:

- whether a sponsor and provider actually need it and will pilot it;
- which narrow service has checkable delivery conditions;
- who verifies delivery and how buyer refusal, verifier failure, and disputes are handled;
- how pending reservations, payment-time debt, release, and cancellation preserve Float accounting;
- whether the benefit justifies added cost, latency, security review, and onboarding steps.

Founder concern: this extension could make Float feel more like a launchpad or marketplace and dilute its purpose. Preserve Shadow's identity as bounded USDC authority for agents; do not introduce a launchpad, provider marketplace, or general arbitration product through this note.

No implementation, deployment, new completion gate, or delivery commitment is authorized by recording these ideas. Path A remains first; Path B remains the subsequent customer-backed DeFi direction. Revisit each optional Float extension separately after the founder has considered it.

## Path B — Expand Into Protocol-Owned DeFi Liquidity

Path B applies Shadow's mandate authority to capital that an organization delegates for protocol operations. It begins after the Path A gate, not in parallel with unfinished Float validation.

### Product hypothesis

An Arc issuer, protocol treasury, vault operator, or liquidity manager will delegate a recurring DeFi operation to an agent or keeper only if the organization can enforce asset, venue, function, amount, slippage, expiry, frequency, and recovery limits before capital moves.

### Customer discovery questions

- Which liquidity operation is already performed repeatedly?
- Who performs it today and what wallet authority do they receive?
- Which failure or abuse scenario prevents broader automation?
- Which rules must be enforced onchain rather than monitored afterward?
- Will the organization provide its own wallet, workflow, test liquidity, and technical owner for a pilot?
- Is the first required venue Uniswap, Morpho, Aave, or another Arc protocol?

### Promotion gate

Shadow implements one real adapter only when a design partner provides:

- a repeated operation with a named economic owner;
- exact policy requirements and failure cases;
- a technical point of contact;
- a wallet and safe test-liquidity plan;
- a written or publicly attributable pilot commitment;
- an agreed success metric and decision date.

Compliments, introductions, grant discussions, generic ecosystem interest, and style adapters do not satisfy this gate.

### First implementation shape

The first promoted workflow must remain narrow:

1. The organization creates a Shadow mandate for one protocol action.
2. The agent proposes the action within the mandate.
3. Shadow verifies the exact target, function, asset, amount, limits, nonce, and expiry.
4. An allowed action reaches the named protocol; a mutated or excessive action is refused before funds move.
5. The execution and policy decision bind to one verifiable receipt.
6. The design partner evaluates whether the workflow should repeat with real operational responsibility.

The existing V4-style and Morpho-style adapters remain simulations until replaced by a real integration and customer-operated pilot.

## Product Boundary

Shadow owns:

- delegated USDC authority;
- sponsor and organization capital limits;
- mandate creation and verification;
- capacity, debt, repayment, and reserve accounting;
- pre-execution ALLOW/BLOCK decisions;
- receipts that bind authorization to the resulting capital action.

Shadow does not become a generic transaction-construction, fork-simulation, signing, retry, or cross-chain execution engine. Infrastructure providers may supply those capabilities later when doing so preserves the Shadow mandate and customer relationship. No Aomi integration is planned for the accelerator roadmap.

## Explicit Exclusions

- No meme launchpad.
- No Robinhood deployment.
- No simultaneous Uniswap, Morpho, and Aave integration spree.
- No generic DAO treasury dashboard.
- No pooled customer capital.
- No token.
- No production DeFi claim based on the current style adapters.
- No institutional-adoption claim before a qualifying customer commitment.
- No real-value mainnet launch before independent security and operating gates pass.
- No return to copy trading as Shadow's lead product.

## Accelerator Narrative

Shadow Float gives autonomous agents bounded USDC purchasing capacity. Sponsors control where money can go; agents sign each request; Shadow pays approved providers, refuses violations, records debt, and restores capacity after repayment. Copy trading proved the mandate primitive. During the accelerator, Shadow will first prove repeated agent commerce, then extend the same enforceable authority to one customer-backed protocol-liquidity operation.

Shadow
└── Enforceable USDC authority for autonomous agents

## Investor-Readiness Test

At the end of the two-month programme, the company is ready for a serious investment conversation only if it can present:

- a target customer with a repeated capital-delegation problem;
- independent use or a committed customer pilot;
- a product action that moves or refuses USDC under enforceable limits;
- a credible path from activity to fees;
- explicit capital and loss ownership;
- evidence that Shadow's mandate and accounting primitive is harder to replace than its interface;
- a focused next market, partner, and 90-day use-of-funds plan.

The accelerator entry establishes access and credibility. Customer commitment, repeat usage, operational readiness, and defensible capital controls establish the company.
